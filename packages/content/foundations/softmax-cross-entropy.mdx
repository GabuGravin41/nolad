### Logits and the softmax

A classifier for $K$ mutually exclusive classes ends in a layer that outputs $K$ real numbers $z = (z_1, \dots, z_K) \in \mathbb{R}^K$, called **logits**. The **softmax** function converts them into a probability vector $p \in (0,1)^K$:

$$
p_k = \frac{e^{z_k}}{Z}, \qquad Z = \sum_{j=1}^{K} e^{z_j}, \qquad k = 1, \dots, K .
$$

Each $p_k$ is positive because the exponential is positive, and $\sum_k p_k = Z/Z = 1$. A larger logit gives a larger probability.

**Worked example.** For $K = 3$ and $z = (2.0,\ 1.0,\ 0.1)$: $e^{2.0} = 7.389$, $e^{1.0} = 2.718$, $e^{0.1} = 1.105$, so $Z = 11.212$ and

$$
p = (0.659,\ 0.242,\ 0.099).
$$

**Invariance to a shift.** Adding a constant $a$ to every logit multiplies every exponential, and therefore $Z$, by $e^{a}$:

$$
\frac{e^{z_k + a}}{\sum_j e^{z_j + a}} = \frac{e^{a}\,e^{z_k}}{e^{a}\,Z} = p_k .
$$

Implementations use this with $a = -\max_j z_j$, so that the largest exponent is 0 and no exponential overflows.

**Two classes.** For $K = 2$, dividing numerator and denominator of $p_1$ by $e^{z_1}$ gives

$$
p_1 = \frac{e^{z_1}}{e^{z_1} + e^{z_2}} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(z_1 - z_2)}} = \sigma(z_1 - z_2),
$$

where $\sigma(t) = 1/(1 + e^{-t})$ is the logistic sigmoid. A two-class softmax is a sigmoid of the logit difference.

### Categorical cross-entropy

Let the true class be $c$ and represent it by the one-hot vector $y$ with $y_c = 1$ and $y_k = 0$ for $k \ne c$. The model assigns probability $p_c$ to the observed class, so the negative log-likelihood of one example is

$$
\ell_{\mathrm{CE}} = -\sum_{k=1}^{K} y_k \log p_k = -\log p_c .
$$

The loss is 0 when $p_c = 1$ and grows without bound as $p_c \to 0$. In the worked example with true class $c = 1$, $\ell_{\mathrm{CE}} = -\log 0.659 = 0.417$. Over a dataset, the loss is averaged over examples.

### Gradient with respect to the logits

*Step 1: take the logarithm of the softmax.* From $p_c = e^{z_c}/Z$,

$$
\log p_c = z_c - \log Z, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \ell = -z_c + \log Z .
$$

*Step 2: differentiate $\log Z$.* Only the $k$-th term of $Z$ depends on $z_k$, and its derivative is $e^{z_k}$:

$$
\frac{\partial \log Z}{\partial z_k} = \frac{1}{Z}\,\frac{\partial Z}{\partial z_k} = \frac{e^{z_k}}{Z} = p_k .
$$

*Step 3: differentiate $-z_c$.* This gives $-1$ if $k = c$ and 0 otherwise, which is $-y_k$.

*Step 4: add the two parts.*

$$
\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial z_k} = p_k - y_k .
$$

The gradient is the prediction minus the target, for every class at once. Gradient descent subtracts a multiple of it, which raises the logit of the true class (where $p_c - 1 \lt 0$) and lowers every other logit in proportion to the probability wrongly assigned to it. In the worked example the gradient is $(0.659 - 1,\ 0.242,\ 0.099) = (-0.341,\ 0.242,\ 0.099)$. Its components sum to zero, consistent with the invariance to a common shift of the logits.

### Soft targets

The derivation used only $\sum_k y_k = 1$ in step 3 once it is written for a general $y$: the derivative of $-\sum_k y_k \log p_k = -\sum_k y_k z_k + \big(\sum_k y_k\big)\log Z$ with respect to $z_k$ is $-y_k + p_k$. The loss

$$
\ell = -\sum_{k=1}^{K} y_k \log p_k
$$

is therefore defined, with the same gradient $p - y$, for any probability vector $y$: a smoothed label, or the fraction of experts who chose each class. It is the cross-entropy between the target distribution $y$ and the prediction $p$, and it equals the KL divergence $\mathrm{KL}(y \,\Vert\, p)$ plus the entropy of $y$, which does not depend on the model. Its minimum over $p$ is at $p = y$, where the gradient vanishes.

### Softmax versus independent sigmoids

A softmax couples the classes: raising one logit lowers every other probability, because the probabilities sum to one. It models a single class per example. Independent sigmoids $p_k = \sigma(z_k)$, each trained with binary cross-entropy, model each class as a separate yes-or-no question and allow several classes to be present at once.

The choice depends on the training labels as well as on the task. In BirdCLEF 2024 a test recording could contain several of the 182 bird species, which suggests independent sigmoids. The winners reported that softmax cross-entropy worked much better than binary cross-entropy for training, because most training clips had a single label. At inference they applied a sigmoid to each logit of the softmax-trained model, which scores each species independently; in their ablation this raised the private score from 0.544 to 0.588 over the softmax readout.
