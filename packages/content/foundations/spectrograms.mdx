### The short-time Fourier transform

A signal $x[n]$ sampled at rate $f_s$ is cut into overlapping frames of length $N$ (the FFT size), spaced by a hop $H$. Each frame is multiplied by a window $w[n]$ (Hann, for example) to reduce edge artefacts, and Fourier-transformed:

$$
X[m, k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n + mH]\,w[n]\,e^{-2\pi i kn/N}.
$$

$m$ indexes time frames and $k$ frequency bins, with bin $k$ at frequency $k f_s/N$. The **power spectrogram** is $|X[m,k]|^2$. It is usually converted to decibels, $10\log_{10}\big(|X[m,k]|^2 + \epsilon\big)$, which compresses its range of several orders of magnitude.

### Time–frequency trade-off

A long frame separates nearby frequencies but spreads events over its duration; a short frame localises events in time but merges nearby frequencies. The bin spacing is $\Delta f = f_s/N$ and the frame duration $\Delta t = N/f_s$, so $\Delta f \cdot \Delta t = 1$ for every choice of $N$. For audio at $f_s = 32$ kHz with $N = 1024$, $\Delta f = 31.25$ Hz and $\Delta t = 32$ ms. For EEG at $f_s = 200$ Hz, resolving 0.5 Hz requires $N = 400$ samples, a frame of 2 s. Members of the winning HMS team replaced the STFT with wavelet and superlet transforms, which vary the frame length with frequency.

### The mel scale

Pitch perception is roughly logarithmic above a few hundred hertz. The **mel scale**

$$
m = 2595\,\log_{10}\!\left(1 + \frac{f}{700}\right)
$$

is approximately linear below 1 kHz and logarithmic above; $f = 1000$ Hz maps to $m = 2595\log_{10}(1 + 1000/700) \approx 1000$ mels. A **mel spectrogram** sums the power spectrum through triangular filters spaced evenly in mels, producing, for example, 128 bands. The BirdCLEF 2024 winners used $N = 1024$, hop $H = 500$ and 128 mel bands between 40 Hz and 15 kHz at 32 kHz sampling. A 10-second clip has $320\,000$ samples and therefore $320\,000 / 500 = 640$ frames, giving a $128 \times 640$ image.

### Wavelets and superlets

A **continuous wavelet transform** correlates the signal with scaled copies of a short oscillating wavelet. Scales that match low frequencies use long wavelets (good frequency resolution); scales for high frequencies use short ones (good time resolution). The resulting **scalogram** has a resolution that varies with frequency, which suits EEG, in which slow delta rhythms and brief spikes both carry information. **Superlets** (Moca et al., 2021) combine several wavelets with increasing numbers of cycles at each frequency by a geometric mean; one member of the winning HMS team found them better than the STFT (the HMS report gives the equations).

### From spectrogram to CNN input

A spectrogram is a single-channel image with time on one axis and frequency on the other. It is resized, normalised, sometimes stacked with other channels, and passed to an ImageNet-pretrained CNN. Augmentations are adapted to this format: **XY-masking** (SpecAugment) sets random time columns and frequency rows to zero, and horizontal CutMix joins two clips in time.
