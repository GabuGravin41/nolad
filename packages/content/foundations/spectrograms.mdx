### The short-time Fourier transform

A signal $x[n]$ sampled at rate $f_s$ is cut into overlapping frames of length $N$ (the FFT size), spaced by a hop $H$. Each frame is multiplied by a window $w[n]$ (Hann, for example) to reduce edge artefacts, and Fourier-transformed:

$$
X[m, k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n + mH]\,w[n]\,e^{-2\pi i kn/N}.
$$

$m$ indexes time frames and $k$ frequency bins, with bin $k$ at frequency $k f_s/N$. The **power spectrogram** is $|X[m,k]|^2$. Taking a logarithm (decibels) compresses its range to something closer to perception and easier for networks.

### Time–frequency trade-off

A long frame resolves nearby frequencies but blurs when they occur; a short frame localises events in time but smears frequency. Frequency resolution is $f_s/N$ and time resolution is about $N/f_s$; their product is fixed. This limitation is why the HMS winners moved from STFT to wavelets and superlets.

### The mel scale

Pitch perception is roughly logarithmic above a few hundred hertz. The **mel scale**

$$
m = 2595\,\log_{10}\!\left(1 + \frac{f}{700}\right)
$$

is approximately linear below 1 kHz and logarithmic above. A **mel spectrogram** sums the power spectrum through triangular filters spaced evenly in mels, producing, for example, 128 bands. The BirdCLEF 2024 winners used $n_{\text{fft}}=1024$, hop 500, 128 mel bands between 40 Hz and 15 kHz at 32 kHz sampling, so a 10-second clip became a $128\times640$ image.

### Wavelets and superlets

A **continuous wavelet transform** correlates the signal with scaled copies of a short oscillating wavelet. Scales that match low frequencies use long wavelets (good frequency resolution); scales for high frequencies use short ones (good time resolution). The resulting **scalogram** adapts its resolution to frequency, which suits EEG, where slow rhythmic delta activity and sharp spikes carry different information. **Superlets** (Moca et al., 2021) combine several wavelets of increasing cycle count at each frequency to sharpen resolution further; one HMS team member found them clearly better than STFT.

### From spectrogram to CNN input

A spectrogram is a single-channel image with time on one axis and frequency on the other. It is resized, normalised, sometimes stacked with other channels, and given to an ImageNet-pretrained CNN. Augmentations are adapted: **XY-masking** (SpecAugment) blanks random time columns and frequency rows; horizontal CutMix splices two clips in time.
