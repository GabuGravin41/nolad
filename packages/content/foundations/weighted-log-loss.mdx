### Definition

A weighted log loss multiplies the log loss of each row $i$ by a weight $w_i > 0$ and normalises by the total weight:

$$
L = \frac{\sum_{i} w_i\, \ell_i}{\sum_{i} w_i}, \qquad
\ell_i = -\big[\,y_i \log p_i + (1 - y_i) \log(1 - p_i)\,\big],
$$

where $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is the label and $p_i \in (0, 1)$ the predicted probability. In RSNA competitions the weight usually depends on which label a row refers to (an "any injury" or "any haemorrhage" row counts more than a subtype row), on the true class (a severe finding counts more than a normal one), or on both.

### Weights that depend on the true class

Let positive rows carry weight $w_1$ and negative rows weight $w_0$. Consider a group of examples that the model cannot distinguish, of which a fraction $\pi$ is positive, and a common prediction $q$. Up to a positive constant that does not depend on $q$, the contribution of the group to the numerator of $L$ is

$$
f(q) = -\big[\,w_1 \pi \log q + w_0 (1 - \pi) \log(1 - q)\,\big].
$$

Its derivative is

$$
f'(q) = -\frac{w_1 \pi}{q} + \frac{w_0 (1 - \pi)}{1 - q}.
$$

Setting $f'(q) = 0$ gives $w_1 \pi (1 - q) = w_0 (1 - \pi)\, q$, and solving for $q$,

$$
q^\star = \frac{w_1 \pi}{w_1 \pi + w_0 (1 - \pi)} .
$$

Since $f''(q) = w_1\pi/q^2 + w_0(1-\pi)/(1-q)^2 > 0$, this is the minimum. (The denominator $\sum_i w_i$ also depends on the labels but not on the predictions, so it does not change the minimiser.)

For $w_1 = w_0$, $q^\star = \pi$: the calibrated probability. For $w_1 = 4$, $w_0 = 1$ and $\pi = 0.05$,

$$
q^\star = \frac{4 \times 0.05}{4 \times 0.05 + 1 \times 0.95} = \frac{0.20}{1.15} \approx 0.174,
$$

more than three times the true frequency. A model trained with unweighted log loss estimates $\pi$ and is therefore scored as under-confident on the positive class. Solutions either train with the same weights as the metric or rescale the predictions afterwards. In terms of the logit $\log\frac{q}{1-q}$, the rescaling adds a constant:

$$
\log\frac{q^\star}{1 - q^\star} = \log\frac{\pi}{1 - \pi} + \log\frac{w_1}{w_0}.
$$

The second-place solution to the RSNA lumbar spine competition multiplied the highest severe-stenosis prediction by 1.25, which is a rescaling of this kind. The multi-class version of the result, $p_k^\star \propto w_k q_k$, is derived in the abdominal trauma report.

### Weights that depend on the label type

If the weight depends only on which label a row describes, and not on its true value, it does not change the optimal prediction for that label: the factor $w$ multiplies $f(q)$ and does not move its minimiser. It changes how much the errors on that label contribute to the average. The winners of the aneurysm and abdominal trauma competitions built dedicated model heads for the heavily weighted "any" labels for this reason.

### Clipping

Most competition implementations clip predictions to $[\epsilon, 1 - \epsilon]$ before taking logarithms, for example with $\epsilon = 10^{-15}$, so that a prediction of exactly 0 or 1 does not give an infinite loss. The clipped loss for a confident error is still very large: $-\ln(10^{-15}) \approx 34.5$, compared with $-\ln(0.5) \approx 0.69$ for an uninformative prediction.
