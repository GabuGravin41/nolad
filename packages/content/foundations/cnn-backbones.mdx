### Residual connections (ResNet)

He et al. (2016) added the input of a block to its output:

$$
y = x + F(x).
$$

In the backward pass, $\partial y/\partial x = I + \partial F/\partial x$. The identity term gives the gradient a direct path through every block, so gradients neither vanish nor explode as depth grows, and networks of 50 to 150 layers train reliably. If a block has nothing useful to add, it can learn $F \approx 0$ and pass its input through.

### Normalisation

**Batch normalisation** standardises each channel using the mean and variance over the current batch, then applies a learned scale and shift:

$$
\hat x = \frac{x-\mu_B}{\sqrt{\sigma_B^2+\epsilon}}, \qquad y = \gamma\hat x + \beta .
$$

It stabilises training but depends on batch statistics, so it degrades when the batch is tiny. The HuBMAP winner, forced to batch size one by memory, replaced it with **group normalisation**, which normalises over groups of channels within each example and does not depend on the batch. **Layer normalisation** does the same over all channels and is standard in transformers and ConvNeXt.

### Squeeze-and-excitation (SE)

An SE block (Hu et al., 2018) lets a network reweight its own channels. It averages each channel over space (squeeze), passes the resulting vector through a small two-layer network ending in a sigmoid (excitation), and multiplies each channel by its weight:

$$
s = \sigma\big(W_2\,\mathrm{ReLU}(W_1\,\bar x)\big), \qquad y_c = s_c\,x_c .
$$

SE-ResNeXt networks, common in 2019–2020 solutions, combine this with grouped convolutions.

### EfficientNet

Tan and Le (2019) scaled depth, width and input resolution together by fixed ratios, producing a family B0 to B7 whose accuracy per FLOP was far better than earlier networks. EfficientNet-B0 has about 5 million parameters; B7 about 66 million. Because B0 and B3 are cheap, they dominate solutions with tight inference budgets such as BirdCLEF's CPU limit.

### ConvNeXt

Liu et al. (2022) modernised the ResNet design with ideas borrowed from vision transformers: large $7\times7$ depthwise convolutions, layer normalisation, GELU activations and fewer normalisation layers. ConvNeXt-Small and ConvNeXt-Tiny appear in the mammography, lumbar spine and HMS winners, often outperforming larger models on small medical datasets.

### Pretraining

Every one of these backbones is usually initialised from weights trained on ImageNet (1.3 million natural photographs, 1,000 classes) or ImageNet-21k. Medical images look nothing like photographs, yet pretrained low-level filters (edges, textures) transfer and training converges faster and generalises better than from random weights. When pretrained weights carry a licence (for example non-commercial), that licence follows every model fine-tuned from them.
