### What a CT scanner measures

Computed tomography sends X-rays through the body from many angles and reconstructs, for every voxel, the linear attenuation coefficient $\mu$: how much the tissue in that voxel weakens the beam. The result is a stack of axial slices, typically 0.5 to 5 mm thick, each a 512×512 grid.

### The Hounsfield scale

Attenuation is reported relative to water and air:

$$
\mathrm{HU} = 1000\times\frac{\mu - \mu_{\text{water}}}{\mu_{\text{water}} - \mu_{\text{air}}} .
$$

By construction water is 0 HU and air is −1000 HU. Approximate values for other tissues:

| Tissue | HU |
| --- | --- |
| Air | −1000 |
| Fat | −100 to −50 |
| Water, cerebrospinal fluid | 0 to 15 |
| Brain (grey and white matter) | 20 to 45 |
| Fresh blood, acute haemorrhage | 50 to 90 |
| Contrast-filled vessels (CT angiography) | 200 to 500 |
| Cortical bone | 700 to 3000 |

Scanners store raw integers; the DICOM header fields `RescaleSlope` and `RescaleIntercept` convert them: $\mathrm{HU} = \text{slope}\times\text{stored} + \text{intercept}$. Forgetting this step silently corrupts every downstream intensity, and it is the first line of most competition preprocessing code.

### Windowing

The scale spans about 4,000 HU; a display, or a network's 8-bit input channel, shows perhaps 256 levels. A **window** with centre (level) $c$ and width $w$ maps HU linearly to $[0,1]$ over the interval $[c-w/2,\ c+w/2]$ and clips outside it:

$$
I = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{HU} - (c - w/2)}{w},\ 0,\ 1\right).
$$

A narrow brain window (centre 40, width 80) spreads the 0–80 HU range of brain and blood across the full intensity scale, so a 30 HU difference between blood and brain becomes a large contrast. A wide bone window compresses soft tissue to grey and shows fractures. Radiologists switch windows constantly. Feeding a network several windows as separate channels gives it the same views, and the 2019 RSNA solutions established brain, subdural and bone windows as a standard three-channel input for head CT.

Other standard windows in these reports: soft tissue or abdomen (centre ~40–50, width ~350–400), mediastinum (50, 350), lung (−600, 1500), and a pulmonary-embolism window (100, 700) that shows contrast-filled arteries and the darker clots within them.

### Slices, series and orientation

A CT **study** contains one or more **series** (reconstructions with different kernels or slice thicknesses). Slices must be ordered by the z-component of `ImagePositionPatient`, not by file name or instance number, and slice thickness can vary between series and scanners. The 2019 haemorrhage winner fed the difference between consecutive z-positions to his sequence model as a feature, because thin and thick slices differ in noise.
