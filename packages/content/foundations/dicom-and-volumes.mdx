### DICOM fields

Medical images are distributed as DICOM files, usually one file per slice. Each file contains pixel data and a header of tagged fields. The fields used in these reports:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `StudyInstanceUID`, `SeriesInstanceUID`, `SOPInstanceUID` | identifiers of the study, the series and the individual image |
| `ImagePositionPatient` | $(x, y, z)$ of the centre of the first pixel, in millimetres, in patient coordinates |
| `ImageOrientationPatient` | direction cosines $\mathbf{r}$ of the image rows and $\mathbf{c}$ of the columns |
| `PixelSpacing`, `SliceThickness` | in-plane pixel spacing and nominal slice thickness, in millimetres |
| `RescaleSlope`, `RescaleIntercept` | convert stored integers to physical units (Hounsfield units for CT) |
| `WindowCenter`, `WindowWidth` | display window suggested by the scanner |
| `PhotometricInterpretation` | whether high values are displayed bright (MONOCHROME2) or dark (MONOCHROME1) |

### Building a volume

1. **Group** files by `SeriesInstanceUID`.
2. **Order** the slices. The slice normal is the cross product of the row and column directions, $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{c}$. The position of slice $i$ along the normal is the scalar projection
   $$
   s_i = \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{p}_i ,
   $$
   where $\mathbf{p}_i$ is its `ImagePositionPatient`. Slices are sorted by $s_i$. For axial slices with $\mathbf{r} = (1, 0, 0)$ and $\mathbf{c} = (0, 1, 0)$, $\mathbf{n} = (0, 0, 1)$ and $s_i$ is the $z$ coordinate.
3. **Rescale** each stored value $v$ to physical units, $u = \text{slope} \cdot v + \text{intercept}$. For CT, typical values are slope 1 and intercept $-1024$.
4. **Stack** the slices into an array of shape (slices, rows, columns) and record the voxel spacing: `PixelSpacing` in plane and the median of the differences $s_{i+1} - s_i$ between slices. The slice spacing can differ from `SliceThickness`, since slices may overlap or have gaps.
5. Optionally **resample** to a fixed spacing and **reorient** to a standard anatomical orientation.

For example, a head CT with 0.45 mm pixels and 0.8 mm slice spacing has voxels of $0.45 \times 0.45 \times 0.8 = 0.162$ mm³; resampling it to 1 mm isotropic spacing reduces the number of voxels by a factor of about $1/0.162 \approx 6.2$.

### Common failures

Slices of different sizes within one series; duplicate positions; missing slices, which appear as a jump in $s_{i+1} - s_i$; multi-frame files that contain a whole series in one file; missing header fields; and inverted photometric interpretation in mammograms. Tools used by the winners include `pydicom`, `dicomsdl` (fast decoding), NVIDIA DALI (decoding on the GPU), `dcm2niix` (conversion to NIfTI) and `SimpleITK`.

### Decoding time

Decoding thousands of compressed DICOM files can take longer than running the model. Several solutions decoded images on the GPU or cached preprocessed arrays; the winner of the RSNA breast cancer competition decoded on the GPU to fit within the 9-hour limit.
