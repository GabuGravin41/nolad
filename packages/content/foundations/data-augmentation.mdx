### Principle

Each time a training example is loaded, it is transformed at random, for example flipped, rotated, scaled, shifted, brightened, blurred or partly masked. The label is kept, or transformed in the same way as the input (a segmentation mask is flipped together with its image). A transformation $g$ is a valid augmentation for a task if the correct output for $g(x)$ is the correspondingly transformed correct output for $x$. Training on transformed examples encourages the model to be invariant (or equivariant) to these transformations, and increases the effective variety of the training data.

### Common families

- **Geometric:** flips, 90° rotations, small rotations, scaling, shearing, elastic and grid distortions.
- **Photometric:** changes of brightness, contrast, gamma, hue and saturation; contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalisation (CLAHE); blur; noise.
- **Erasing:** Cutout and CoarseDropout set random rectangles to a constant; XY-masking sets random rows and columns of a spectrogram to zero.
- **Mixing:** **Mixup** blends two examples and their labels with a coefficient $\lambda \in [0, 1]$, usually drawn from a Beta distribution:
  $$
  \tilde x = \lambda\, x_a + (1 - \lambda)\, x_b, \qquad \tilde y = \lambda\, y_a + (1 - \lambda)\, y_b .
  $$
  **CutMix** pastes a rectangle from $x_b$ into $x_a$ and mixes the labels in proportion to area: if the rectangle covers a fraction $r$ of the image, $\tilde y = (1 - r)\, y_a + r\, y_b$.

### Domain-specific augmentation

Several augmentations in these reports are derived from how the data are produced:

- **Stain variation** in pathology (HuBMAP): tissue from different laboratories is stained differently, and training images are recoloured to other stain profiles.
- **Finger dropout** in sign language (ASL fingerspelling): MediaPipe sometimes fails to detect fingers, and randomly zeroing fingers during training produces the same pattern.
- **Left–right flip with label exchange** (aneurysm, EEG): flipping a brain exchanges left and right arteries or hemispheres, so the corresponding labels are exchanged as well. Without the exchange, the flip would corrupt side-specific labels.
- **Simulated low resolution** (aneurysm): some scans have thick slices, and downsampling followed by upsampling of training volumes reproduces their appearance.
- **Coordinate and slice jitter** (lumbar spine): the second stage receives crops located by a first-stage model that is sometimes off by one slice; the training crops are shifted with the error rates measured for the first stage.
- **Time-wise CutMix within one signer** (ASL fingerspelling): two sequences are cut at the same fraction of their length and joined, and their phrases are cut and joined at the same fraction.

### Augmentations that change the answer

An augmentation that changes the correct output trains the model on wrong labels. A vertical flip of a chest radiograph produces an anatomy that does not occur; strong colour changes can remove the cue that distinguishes two skin lesions; mirroring a brain without exchanging left and right labels assigns the wrong side. Several write-ups list augmentations that were tried and removed.
