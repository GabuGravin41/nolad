### Architecture

Segmentation requires an output with the same spatial size as the input, one label per pixel. A classification network reduces the resolution in stages and ends with a small grid of features. Ronneberger et al. (2015) combined such an **encoder** with a symmetric **decoder**:

1. **Encoder.** Each stage applies convolutions and then halves the resolution, usually doubling the number of channels. After $S$ stages, an input of $H \times W$ pixels becomes a feature map of $H/2^{S} \times W/2^{S}$ cells, each summarising a large region of the input.
2. **Decoder.** Each stage doubles the resolution, by interpolation or transposed convolution, and usually halves the number of channels.
3. **Skip connections.** Each decoder stage concatenates its input with the encoder feature map of the same resolution. The decoder thereby receives both the features of the deep stages and the spatial detail of the early stages, which is needed to place boundaries.
4. **Output head.** A $1 \times 1$ convolution maps the feature vector of each pixel to class logits.

For example, with a $512 \times 512$ input and $S = 5$ stages with channel counts 64, 128, 256, 512 and 1024, the deepest feature map has $512 / 2^5 = 16$ cells per side and 1024 channels, and the decoder restores $512 \times 512$ in five doublings.

In practice the encoder is usually a pretrained classification backbone (ResNet-34, EfficientNet-B3, ConvNeXt), and libraries such as `segmentation_models_pytorch` or MONAI provide the decoder. The same design applies in 3D with 3D convolutions, where each downsampling reduces the number of voxels by a factor of 8.

### Variants in the reports

- **UNet++, FPN, UPerNet and DeepLabV3** differ in how features from different resolutions are combined.
- **nnU-Net** (Isensee et al., 2021) is a U-Net framework that sets the patch size, voxel spacing, normalisation, augmentation and training schedule automatically from properties of the dataset. It was used for the segmentation stages of the winning aneurysm solution and is a common baseline for 3D medical segmentation.
- **Auxiliary segmentation heads** attached to the encoder of a classifier (abdominal trauma, aneurysm) are trained with segmentation targets but do not produce the final output; the authors report that they improve classification.
- **Truncated decoders.** The CZII cryo-ET winners found the output of the penultimate decoder stage, at half resolution, more accurate for locating particle centres than the full-resolution output, and removed the last stage.

### Losses

Segmentation models are usually trained with pixel-wise cross-entropy, an overlap loss such as soft Dice, or their sum. The Dice primer derives the overlap losses and their gradients.
