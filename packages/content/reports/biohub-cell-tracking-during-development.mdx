## Scientific background

In developmental biology, understanding how a fertilised egg transforms into a complex organism requires tracking every cell as it divides, moves and differentiates. The zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) is a primary vertebrate model for this study: its embryos develop externally and are optically transparent, allowing optical microscopes to capture whole-embryo cell dynamics in vivo.

<Deep title="Light-sheet microscopy and volumetric embryogenesis" kind="domain">

1. **Light-sheet illumination.** Conventional confocal microscopes illuminate the entire specimen along the optical axis, causing rapid photobleaching and phototoxicity. Light-sheet fluorescence microscopy (LSFM) illuminates only a thin plane (typically 1–3 µm) perpendicular to the detection objective. By stepping the light sheet through the sample, an entire 3D volume is recorded with minimal light exposure.
2. **Volumetric time-lapse ($4\text{D}$).** Repeating the 3D scan every minute over 12 to 24 hours produces a 4D array $(T, Z, Y, X)$ of hundreds of gigabytes. Each frame captures thousands of fluorescently labelled cell nuclei.
3. **Mitosis and lineage trees.** Cells continuously undergo mitosis: a single parent cell divides into two daughter cells. Reconstructing these relationships over time yields a directed temporal tree—the cell lineage graph.
4. **The annotation bottleneck.** A single embryonic volume contains $10^3$ to $10^5$ cells. Manually tracking even a hundred cells across hundreds of timepoints requires tens of hours of manual curation. Automating this reconstruction is the core goal of the Chan Zuckerberg Biohub challenge.
</Deep>

Cell tracking in dense tissues poses three physical difficulties:
- **Crowding and deformation:** Nuclei crowd closely together, deform, and undergo sudden spatial shifts during tissue folding.
- **Signal-to-noise attenuation:** Fluorescence signal decays with depth in thick tissue due to light scattering and absorption.
- **Sparse annotation:** Because dense manual tracking is impossible, ground-truth annotations cover only a tiny fraction of the lineages present in the data.

## Data

The competition dataset consisted of time-lapse 3D volumes of zebrafish embryogenesis provided by the Royer Group at the Chan Zuckerberg Biohub San Francisco.

Each recording was stored as an OME-Zarr array with spatial coordinates $(Z, Y, X)$ across discrete time steps $T$:

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| $T$ | Time points (roughly 100 frames per video sequence) |
| $Z$ | Axial slices (typically 60 to 120 planes per volume) |
| $Y, X$ | Lateral field of view (typically $512 \times 512$ to $1024 \times 1024$ voxels) |
| Voxel size | Anisotropic, with axial resolution lower than lateral resolution |

### Ground-truth sparsity

The ground truth provided by human curators covered only about **2.8%** of all cells in the embryo. The remaining ~97.2% of cells were unlabelled.

Submissions represented the lineage graph in two CSV tables:
1. **Nodes table:** Detections with coordinates: `dataset`, `node_id`, `t`, `z`, `y`, `x`.
2. **Edges table:** Directed temporal links: `dataset`, `source_id`, `target_id`.

A node at time $t$ can have:
- At most 1 incoming edge from time $t-1$ (a cell cannot have two distinct biological origins).
- At most 2 outgoing edges to time $t+1$ (either a single continuity edge, or 2 division edges indicating mitosis).

## Evaluation metric

The evaluation metric scores both cell continuity (tracking links) and mitotic events (divisions), while accounting for the extreme sparsity of the annotations.

The final competition score is:

$$
\text{Score} = J_{\mathrm{adj}} + 0.1 \times J_{\mathrm{div}}
$$

where $J_{\mathrm{adj}}$ is the **Adjusted Edge Jaccard** index and $J_{\mathrm{div}}$ is the **Division Jaccard** index.

### Adjusted Edge Jaccard ($J_{\mathrm{adj}}$)

Let $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{gt}}$ be the set of annotated ground-truth edges and $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{pred}}$ the set of predicted edges. An edge is formed by a pair of nodes $(u, v)$ with $t_v = t_u + 1$. A predicted edge is matched to a ground-truth edge if both the source and target spatial locations fall within an acceptance distance tolerance $\delta$:

$$
J_{\mathrm{edge}} = \frac{|\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{pred}} \cap \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{gt}}|}{|\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{pred}} \cup \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{gt}}|} = \frac{\mathrm{TP}_{\mathrm{edge}}}{\mathrm{TP}_{\mathrm{edge}} + \mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{edge}} + \mathrm{FN}_{\mathrm{edge}}}
$$

Because ground truth is sparse, evaluating $J_{\mathrm{edge}}$ only on annotated subgraphs could allow degenerate strategies (such as hallucinating millions of dense edges or pruning everything outside known tracks). To ensure global balance, the organisers introduced a graph-size penalty:

$$
J_{\mathrm{adj}} = \max\left(0,\, J_{\mathrm{edge}} \times \left(1 - 0.1 \times \frac{|N_{\mathrm{pred}} - N_{\mathrm{est}}|}{N_{\mathrm{est}}}\right)\right)
$$

where:
- $N_{\mathrm{pred}}$ is the total number of predicted nodes in the submission.
- $N_{\mathrm{est}}$ is the organisers' estimated true number of cells in the entire volume.

If a model predicts 50% more or fewer nodes than $N_{\mathrm{est}}$, the factor is $1 - 0.1 \times 0.5 = 0.95$, shrinking the edge Jaccard by 5%.

### Division Jaccard ($J_{\mathrm{div}}$)

A cell division event occurs when a parent node $p$ at frame $t$ connects to two distinct daughter nodes $d_1, d_2$ at frame $t+1$:

$$
\text{Division: } (p, d_1) \in \mathcal{E} \quad \text{and} \quad (p, d_2) \in \mathcal{E}, \quad d_1 \neq d_2
$$

A predicted division is a true positive $\mathrm{TP}_{\mathrm{div}}$ if parent $p$ and both daughters $d_1, d_2$ match the corresponding ground-truth parent and daughters within spatial tolerance. Unmatched predicted divisions are $\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{div}}$, and missed ground-truth divisions are $\mathrm{FN}_{\mathrm{div}}$:

$$
J_{\mathrm{div}} = \frac{\mathrm{TP}_{\mathrm{div}}}{\mathrm{TP}_{\mathrm{div}} + \mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{div}} + \mathrm{FN}_{\mathrm{div}}}
$$

The division term carries weight $0.1$, contributing up to 0.10 to the total score of approximately 1.10.

<Deep title="Graph matching under sparse ground truth" kind="math">

Consider a movie with $N_{\mathrm{est}} = 10{,}000$ estimated nodes, where human annotators tracked 280 nodes forming 270 true edges ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{gt}}$) and 20 true divisions.

**Submission 1 (Precise but incomplete):**
The model outputs only the high-confidence subgraph: 260 true edges, 10 false edges, 18 true divisions, 2 false divisions, and $N_{\mathrm{pred}} = 5{,}000$ nodes.
- $\mathrm{TP}_{\mathrm{edge}} = 260$, $\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{edge}} = 10$, $\mathrm{FN}_{\mathrm{edge}} = 10$.
- $J_{\mathrm{edge}} = \frac{260}{260 + 10 + 10} = \frac{260}{280} \approx 0.9286$.
- Size deviation: $\frac{|5{,}000 - 10{,}000|}{10{,}000} = 0.50$.
- $J_{\mathrm{adj}} = 0.9286 \times (1 - 0.1 \times 0.50) = 0.9286 \times 0.95 = 0.8822$.
- $J_{\mathrm{div}} = \frac{18}{18 + 2 + 2} = \frac{18}{22} \approx 0.8182$.
- $\text{Score} = 0.8822 + 0.1 \times 0.8182 = 0.9640$.

**Submission 2 (Calibrated full volume):**
The model predicts cells across the full volume using pseudo-labels, detecting $N_{\mathrm{pred}} = 9{,}800$ nodes, with 265 true edges and 12 false edges in the annotated regions, plus 18 true divisions and 3 false divisions:
- $J_{\mathrm{edge}} = \frac{265}{265 + 12 + 5} = \frac{265}{282} \approx 0.9397$.
- Size deviation: $\frac{|9{,}800 - 10{,}000|}{10{,}000} = 0.02$.
- $J_{\mathrm{adj}} = 0.9397 \times (1 - 0.1 \times 0.02) = 0.9397 \times 0.998 = 0.9378$.
- $J_{\mathrm{div}} = \frac{18}{18 + 3 + 2} = \frac{18}{23} \approx 0.7826$.
- $\text{Score} = 0.9378 + 0.1 \times 0.7826 = 1.0161$.

Calibration to the estimated global density $N_{\mathrm{est}}$ is necessary to avoid losing up to $0.05$ points on the node adjustment factor.

### Stage 1: 3D U-Net Detection

Rather than regressing 3D bounding boxes, the detector follows the **Cellpose** formulation (Stringer et al., 2021) extended to 3D.

**Architecture.** A vanilla 3D U-Net processes overlapping $Z \times Y \times X$ subvolume crops. The network outputs two heads:
1. **Foreground probability map:** $p(\mathbf{x}) \in [0, 1]$ indicating whether voxel $\mathbf{x} = (z, y, x)$ belongs to a cell nucleus.
2. **3D Flow field:** $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}) = (v_z, v_y, v_x) \in \mathbb{R}^3$, a unit-length vector field pointing from each voxel toward the centroid of its enclosing nucleus.

<Foundation id="unet" />

**Pretraining and supervision.**
- **Self-supervised 3D MAE:** A lightweight 3D Masked Autoencoder was trained on unlabelled 3D volume patches to learn spatial image representations under variable fluorescence attenuation.
- **Ground-truth stamps:** Supervised fine-tuning used spheres of radius $4.305\ \mu\text{m}$ placed at each annotated ground-truth coordinate.
- **Pseudo-labelling:** After training on the 2.8% annotated nodes, the detector predicted cell positions across unlabelled regions. High-confidence detections were converted into pseudo-labelled stamps, and the U-Net was retrained.

<Foundation id="pseudo-labelling" />

**Centre extraction.** Centroids are identified by tracing voxels along the flow field:

$$
\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{x}_k + \eta\,\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}_k)
$$

Voxels converge at local topological sinks corresponding to cell centres. Points with foreground probability $p(\mathbf{x}) > \tau_{\mathrm{fg}}$ form the candidate node set $\mathcal{V}_t = \{\mathbf{c}_i\}_{i=1}^{M_t}$ at each time step $t$.

### Stage 2: Soon Net (Division & Motion Prediction)

Tracking cells through mitosis fails when relying solely on frame-to-frame proximity: during late anaphase and telophase, two daughter cells separate rapidly from the parent location, while the parent nucleus disappears.

Alvarez introduced a specialised model named **Soon Net**:
- For every candidate centre $\mathbf{c}_i$ at frame $t$, a 3D crop of size $(D_z, D_y, D_x)$ is extracted across three consecutive frames: $t-1$, $t$, and $t+1$.
- A compact 3D CNN extracts spatial feature maps from each crop, which are flattened into temporal tokens and processed by a small Transformer encoder.

The model predicts two outputs for each cell:
1. **Mitotic status (3-way classification):**
   - Interphase (non-dividing)
   - Soon to divide (mitosis within 1–2 frames)
   - Just divided (daughter cell separating from division)
2. **Estimated displacement vector:** $\Delta \mathbf{c}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ estimating where the cell will move between $t$ and $t+1$.

### Stage 3: Learned Linker (LightGlue-inspired)

Traditional cell trackers connect detections across time using nearest neighbours or distance-weighted cost matrices. In dense zebrafish regions, nearest-neighbour matching confuses neighbouring cells undergoing rapid cytoplasmic streaming.

Alvarez replaced distance matching with a learned neural linker adapted from **LightGlue** (Lindenberger et al., 2023):
- Let $\mathcal{V}_t = \{\mathbf{u}_i\}$ be the nodes at time $t$ and $\mathcal{V}_{t+1} = \{\mathbf{v}_j\}$ the nodes at time $t+1$.
- Candidate pairs are restricted to pairs within a spatial search radius $R_{\mathrm{search}}$ (typically $15\ \mu\text{m}$).
- Each node $\mathbf{u}_i$ receives a feature vector $\mathbf{h}_i$ encoding:
  - Coordinate position $(z, y, x)$ and local fluorescence intensity.
  - Motion-compensated coordinates $\mathbf{u}_i + \Delta \mathbf{u}_i$ from Soon Net.
  - Soon Net division probability vector.
  - Local density (count of neighbours within radii 5, 10, and 20 µm).

The candidate bipartite graph is processed by a stack of self-attention layers (exchanging information among cells in frame $t$) and cross-attention layers (exchanging information between candidate pairs across $t$ and $t+1$).

The final linear projection computes an edge affinity logit $A_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}$ for every candidate pair $(i, j)$:

$$
S_{ij} = \sigma(A_{ij}) \in [0, 1]
$$

### Stage 4: Lineage Graph Decoding

Given edge affinities $S_{ij}$ and division probabilities $P_{\mathrm{div}}(i)$ from Soon Net, the final lineage graph is constructed via greedy bipartite matching subject to biological validity constraints.

<Deep title="Bipartite graph edge decoding" kind="code">

The following Python function illustrates the greedy assignment logic used to convert edge affinity scores and division states into a valid cell lineage graph:

```python title="decode_lineage_graph.py (A Nolad illustration)"
import numpy as np

def decode_lineage(
    candidate_edges: list[tuple[int, int, float]], 
    division_probs: dict[int, float], 
    div_threshold: float = 0.55, 
    link_threshold: float = 0.40
) -> list[tuple[int, int]]:
    """
    Decodes candidate edges between frame t and t+1 into valid lineage links.
    
    candidate_edges: list of (source_id, target_id, score) sorted by score descending.
    division_probs: mapping of source_id -> probability of undergoing mitosis.
    """
    # Sort candidate edges by score descending
    edges_sorted = sorted(candidate_edges, key=lambda e: e[2], reverse=True)
    
    in_degree = {}   # target_id -> count of incoming edges (max 1)
    out_degree = {}  # source_id -> count of outgoing edges (max 1, or 2 if dividing)
    selected_edges = []
    
    for u, v, score in edges_sorted:
        if score < link_threshold:
            break
            
        is_dividing = division_probs.get(u, 0.0) >= div_threshold
        max_out = 2 if is_dividing else 1
        
        curr_in = in_degree.get(v, 0)
        curr_out = out_degree.get(u, 0)
        
        # Enforce biological constraints
        if curr_in < 1 and curr_out < max_out:
            selected_edges.append((u, v))
            in_degree[v] = curr_in + 1
            out_degree[u] = curr_out + 1
            
    return selected_edges
```

This greedy decoding processes candidate edges in $\mathcal{O}(E \log E)$ time, completing in under 2 seconds per volume frame.
</Deep>

### 3rd Place: yu4u (0.96714)
The third-place solution by **yu4u** combined six stages:
1. **Cell Detection:** Ensembled Gaussian heatmaps from 2.5D U-Nets and a 3D SegResNet backbone to localize cell centers.
2. **Dense 3D Flow:** Estimated volumetric displacement fields between consecutive 3D frames using a 3D optical flow network.
3. **Cell Matching:** Scored candidate correspondences using deep image patch embeddings and motion-corrected coordinate distances.
4. **Division Identification:** Identified dividing parents using original and motion-aligned subvolumes, supported by auxiliary loss heads.
5. **Graph Optimization:** Jointly selected temporal continuity edges and division bifurcations via global graph optimization (min-cost flow).
6. **Post-processing:** Reconnected trajectories spanning single-frame detection drops (gap closing) and pruned isolated short fragments.

### Considerations for deployment

1. **Optical Point Spread Function (PSF):** In light-sheet microscopes, axial resolution ($Z$) is typically 2× to 4× coarser than lateral resolution ($X, Y$). The 3D U-Net and Soon Net operate on anisotropic crops; when deploying on two-photon or spinning-disk confocal systems with different axial ratios, spatial resampling is required.
2. **Division Thresholding:** The competition metric weights the division Jaccard at $0.1$. In biological studies focused strictly on lineage tree topology (such as clonal cell fate tracking), division precision is critical. Deployers should tune the Soon Net division threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{div}}$ to maximize the F1-score of detected bifurcations independently of the competition objective.
