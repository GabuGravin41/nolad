## Clinical background

A pneumothorax is air in the pleural space, the thin space between the lung and the chest wall. The pressure in this space is normally below atmospheric pressure, which holds the lung against the chest wall. When air enters the space, the lung partly or completely collapses. The competition description lists blunt chest injury and underlying lung disease as causes, and a pneumothorax can also occur without an apparent cause. A small pneumothorax may cause mild breathlessness and resolve without treatment. In a tension pneumothorax, air continues to enter the pleural space and displaces the heart and great vessels; this is a medical emergency.

The usual first investigation is a chest radiograph. On a frontal radiograph the edge of the collapsed lung appears as a thin white line, the visceral pleural line, with lung markings on its inner side and only dark air beyond it. The line can be faint, particularly at the apex of the lung, on a radiograph taken with the patient lying down, or where a rib overlaps it. The competition description names two uses for an automated reader: ordering the reporting worklist so that radiographs with a suspected pneumothorax are read first, and supporting clinicians who are not radiologists and have to act before a radiologist is available.

<Deep title="Radiographic signs of pneumothorax" kind="domain">

- **Pleural line.** A fine, sharp white line parallel to the chest wall, with lung markings inside it and none outside it.
- **Location.** On an upright radiograph, air rises and a pneumothorax collects at the apex. On a supine radiograph, which is typical in intensive care, air collects anteriorly and inferiorly, and the signs are less direct, for example an unusually deep and dark angle between the diaphragm and the chest wall.
- **Mimics.** Skin folds, the edge of the scapula and clothing can produce lines that resemble a pleural line.
- **Signs of treatment.** A patient who has already been treated usually has a chest drain, a tube inserted between the ribs. If many positive training images show drains, a model can learn to detect the drain instead of the air. Such a model is of little use for untreated patients, who are the ones a triage system is meant to find.
</Deep>

## Data

The images are frontal chest radiographs of 1024 × 1024 pixels derived from the NIH ChestX-ray14 collection, annotated with pixel masks of pneumothorax for the competition and distributed as DICOM files through the Google Cloud Healthcare API. Masks were given as run-length encodings. An image could contain several separate regions of pneumothorax, or none. Roughly a fifth of the training images contained a pneumothorax.

The competition ran in two stages. Stage 1 ended on 28 August 2019, and teams had to upload the models they would use before it closed. The stage-2 test set was then released and scored with those models, so the final test data could not be used for tuning.

## Evaluation metric

For a predicted mask $X$ and a true mask $Y$, each a set of pixels, the Dice coefficient is

$$
\mathrm{Dice}(X,Y) = \frac{2\,|X\cap Y|}{|X|+|Y|}, \qquad \mathrm{Dice}(\varnothing,\varnothing) = 1 ,
$$

where $|\cdot|$ counts pixels. The second definition sets the value for an image where both masks are empty, for which the fraction is $0/0$. The score is the mean of $\mathrm{Dice}(X,Y)$ over all test images.

The convention for empty masks gives four cases:

| Image | Prediction | Dice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No pneumothorax | empty | 1 |
| No pneumothorax | any pixel | 0 |
| Pneumothorax | empty | 0 |
| Pneumothorax | non-empty | between 0 and 1 |

On an image without pneumothorax, $|Y| = 0$ and $X \cap Y = \varnothing$, so any non-empty prediction gives $0/|X| = 0$. A single false pixel therefore costs the whole point for that image.

<Deep title="Expected score as a function of detection and mask quality" kind="math">

Let $\pi \in [0,1]$ be the fraction of test images that contain a pneumothorax. Describe a model by three quantities:

- $s \in [0,1]$, the sensitivity: the fraction of positive images on which it outputs a non-empty mask;
- $c \in [0,1]$, the specificity: the fraction of negative images on which it outputs an empty mask;
- $d \in [0,1]$, the mean Dice on the positive images on which it outputs a non-empty mask.

By the table above, a negative image scores 1 with probability $c$ and 0 otherwise, so its expected score is $c$. A positive image scores $d$ on average with probability $s$ and 0 otherwise, so its expected score is $s\,d$. Weighting the two groups by their shares of the test set gives

$$
\mathbb{E}[\text{score}] = (1-\pi)\,c + \pi\,s\,d .
$$

The partial derivatives are

$$
\frac{\partial\,\mathbb{E}[\text{score}]}{\partial c} = 1-\pi,
\qquad
\frac{\partial\,\mathbb{E}[\text{score}]}{\partial s} = \pi\,d,
\qquad
\frac{\partial\,\mathbb{E}[\text{score}]}{\partial d} = \pi\,s .
$$

With $\pi = 0.2$, one percentage point of specificity is worth $0.8 \times 0.01 = 0.008$ in score, and one percentage point of sensitivity is worth at most $0.2 \times 0.01 = 0.002$. Specificity is therefore worth at least four times as much per point.

Two worked cases, with $\pi = 0.2$:

- Perfect detection and moderate masks, $s = c = 1$ and $d = 0.6$: $\;0.8 \times 1 + 0.2 \times 1 \times 0.6 = 0.8 + 0.12 = 0.92$.
- Perfect masks and weaker detection, $s = c = 0.9$ and $d = 1$: $\;0.8 \times 0.9 + 0.2 \times 0.9 \times 1 = 0.72 + 0.18 = 0.90$.

The first model scores higher although its masks are much worse.
</Deep>

## Winning solution

The winner, Aimoldin Anuar (Kaggle handle sneddy), competed alone and states in the write-up that this was his first image-segmentation project. The solution consists of U-Nets trained with a combined loss, a sampling schedule that changes the proportion of positive images during training, and a rule with three parameters that converts the probability map into a mask or an empty prediction.

### Models

All models are U-Nets with ImageNet-pretrained encoders:

- **AlbuNet**, a U-Net with a ResNet-34 encoder, from the TernausNet project.
- A U-Net with a **ResNet-50** encoder, and an **SCSE U-Net** with an **SE-ResNeXt-50** encoder, both taken from Selim Seferbekov's SpaceNet 4 solution. SCSE stands for concurrent spatial and channel squeeze-and-excitation: blocks in the decoder that rescale each channel and each spatial position by learned weights.

### Decision rule with three thresholds

A common design, used by the second-place team, trains a separate classifier to decide whether an image contains a pneumothorax and draws a mask only on images the classifier marks as positive. The winner made both decisions from the segmentation output, with three parameters:

- `top_score_threshold`, a high probability threshold used only to decide whether the image is positive;
- `min_contour_area`, the number of pixels that must exceed the high threshold for the image to be declared positive;
- `bottom_score_threshold`, a lower threshold used to draw the mask on images declared positive.

Let $p(u) \in [0,1]$ be the predicted probability at pixel $u$ of an image with pixel set $\Omega$, and write $t_{\mathrm{top}}$, $A_{\min}$ and $t_{\mathrm{bot}}$ for the three parameters. The number of confident pixels is

$$
n_{\mathrm{top}} = \big|\{\, u \in \Omega : p(u) > t_{\mathrm{top}} \,\}\big| ,
$$

and the predicted mask is

$$
X =
\begin{cases}
\varnothing, & n_{\mathrm{top}} < A_{\min}, \\[2pt]
\{\, u \in \Omega : p(u) > t_{\mathrm{bot}} \,\}, & n_{\mathrm{top}} \ge A_{\min}.
\end{cases}
$$

<Deep title="The rule in the winner's README" kind="code" open>

```python title="kaggle-pneumathorax README (simplified version of the rule)"
classification_mask = predicted > top_score_threshold
mask = predicted.copy()
mask[classification_mask.sum(axis=(1,2,3)) < min_contour_area, :,:,:] = np.zeros_like(predicted[0])
mask = mask > bot_score_threshold
return mask
```

- `predicted` is a batch of probability maps with shape `(N, 1, H, W)`.
- `classification_mask` marks the pixels above the high threshold.
- `classification_mask.sum(axis=(1,2,3))` counts those pixels in each image, which is $n_{\mathrm{top}}$. Images with fewer than `min_contour_area` such pixels are set to zero, that is, declared negative.
- For the remaining images, the mask is the set of pixels above the low threshold.
</Deep>

<Source href="https://github.com/sneddy/kaggle-pneumathorax">sneddy/kaggle-pneumathorax, README</Source>

The rule separates the two decisions that the metric rewards differently. On a negative image a single predicted pixel costs a full point, so the image is declared positive only when a region of confident prediction is present. On a positive image the Dice of the mask depends on its boundary, and the lower threshold sets that boundary independently of the detection decision. A single threshold would have to serve both purposes.

The best triplet $(t_{\mathrm{top}}, A_{\min}, t_{\mathrm{bot}})$ on validation data was $(0.75, 2000, 0.3)$, and the best on the public leaderboard was $(0.7, 600, 0.3)$. The final submissions used values between these. If the area is counted at the full resolution of $1024 \times 1024 = 1{,}048{,}576$ pixels, 2000 pixels are about 0.19% of the image and 600 pixels about 0.06%.

### Loss function

The models were trained with a weighted sum of binary cross-entropy (BCE), soft Dice loss and focal loss. For pixel probabilities $p(u)$ and labels $y(u) \in \{0,1\}$ on an image,

$$
\mathcal{L} = \lambda_{\mathrm{BCE}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{Dice}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dice}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{F}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{focal}},
$$

with non-negative weights $\lambda$. The three terms are defined as follows. BCE is the mean over pixels of $-[\,y \log p + (1-y)\log(1-p)\,]$. The soft Dice loss is $1 - \mathrm{Dice}_{\mathrm{soft}}$, where the soft Dice replaces the set sizes in the Dice coefficient by sums of probabilities:

$$
I = \sum_{u} p(u)\,y(u), \qquad P = \sum_u p(u), \qquad G = \sum_u y(u), \qquad \mathrm{Dice}_{\mathrm{soft}} = \frac{2I}{P + G}.
$$

The focal loss multiplies the per-pixel cross-entropy by $(1-p_t)^{\gamma}$, where $p_t$ is the probability assigned to the true class and $\gamma \ge 0$, so that pixels already classified with high confidence contribute less.

The weights $(\lambda_{\mathrm{BCE}}, \lambda_{\mathrm{Dice}}, \lambda_{\mathrm{F}})$ were $(3, 1, 4)$ for the best validation ResNet-34 model and the SE-ResNeXt-50 model, $(1, 1, 1)$ for the ResNet-34 model tuned on the public leaderboard, and $(2, 1, 2)$ for the ResNet-50 model.

<Deep title="Properties of the three loss terms" kind="math">

- **BCE** is a mean over pixels. On an image in which 99% of pixels are background, 99% of its terms come from background pixels, so a small object contributes little to the loss.
- **Soft Dice** is a ratio computed per image, so it depends on the fraction of the object that is covered, not on the object's size in pixels. When the target is empty ($G = 0$), $I = 0$ and $\mathrm{Dice}_{\mathrm{soft}} = 0$ for any $P > 0$, and the loss gives the same value, 1, for a nearly empty and a very wrong prediction. On negative images it therefore carries no useful signal, and the BCE term supplies the gradient.
- **Focal loss** reduces the contribution of confidently classified pixels by the factor $(1-p_t)^\gamma$. For $\gamma = 2$, a pixel with $p_t = 0.9$ is weighted by $0.1^2 = 0.01$ and a pixel with $p_t = 0.6$ by $0.4^2 = 0.16$, so ambiguous pixels, such as those near a faint pleural line, dominate the gradient.

The winner's write-up gives the weights but not a derivation of them; they were selected by validation.
</Deep>

### Sampling of positive images

The **sample rate** $r$ is the fraction of positive images in a training epoch. The winner's data sampler took every positive image in each epoch and added negative images until positives made up the fraction $r$. With $P$ positive images, the number of negative images $N$ satisfies

$$
\frac{P}{P + N} = r ,
$$

and solving for $N$ gives

$$
N = P\,\frac{1-r}{r} .
$$

At $r = 0.8$ this is $N = 0.25\,P$, one negative for every four positives. At $r = 0.4$ it is $N = 1.5\,P$, three negatives for every two positives. In the training set itself roughly one image in five is positive, $r \approx 0.2$.

The rate was 0.8 at the start of training and was lowered to 0.4 by the end. A high rate provides many examples of pneumothorax while the model learns to draw masks. A lower rate later brings the training distribution closer to the test distribution, in which most images are negative and a false positive costs a full point.

### Training

The write-up describes four training phases:

| Phase | Learning rate | Sample rate | Schedule | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Part 0 | about $10^{-3}$–$10^{-4}$ | 0.8 | reduce on plateau, 10–12 epochs | reach validation Dice ≈ 0.835 |
| Part 1 | about $10^{-5}$ | 0.6 | cosine or cosine with warm restarts | train until convergence |
| Part 2 | about $10^{-5}$ | 0.4 | cosine | adapt to the class balance of the test data |
| Second stage | $10^{-5}$–$10^{-6}$ | 0.5 | cosine | fine-tune on the stage-2 training data |

Further details:

- **Resolution.** Models were first trained at 512 × 512 and then fine-tuned at the full 1024 × 1024, with the encoder frozen for the first epochs of the high-resolution phase.
- **Batch size** 2–4, on one or sometimes two GTX 1080 Ti GPUs.
- **Augmentation** with the albumentations library: horizontal flip; one of contrast, gamma or brightness change (probability 0.3); one of elastic, grid or optical distortion (probability 0.3); shift-scale-rotate; resize.
- **Checkpoint averaging.** The three best checkpoints of each fold were averaged.
- **Test-time augmentation.** Horizontal flip.

### Final submissions

The best single configuration on the public leaderboard, the ResNet-34 AlbuNet tuned on the public leaderboard, scored 0.8871 public, higher than any of the winner's ensembles. Both final submissions were nevertheless ensembles, and they scored 0.8679 and 0.8641 on the private leaderboard.

## Second place: a classifier and separate segmentation models

The second-place team X5 (yelan, He, bird, SeuTao, Sen Yang) is the same group that won the 2019 RSNA intracranial haemorrhage competition. Their solution separates classification from segmentation:

- **Classification.** A multi-task U-Net with an added classification branch decides whether each image is positive. The classification branch is trained with BCE plus focal loss and the segmentation output with BCE. Backbones were SE-ResNeXt-50, SE-ResNeXt-101 and EfficientNet-B3, and the classification outputs were combined by stacking.
- **Segmentation.** U-Net and DeepLabV3 models with SE-ResNeXt-50/101 and EfficientNet-B3/B5 backbones, trained with Dice loss on positive images only, draw the masks. Their outputs are averaged.

The segmentation models see only positive images during training, and the decision on negative images is left to the classifier.

## Third place: lung crops and pseudo-labelled external data

bestfitting competed alone.

- **Lung crops.** A first U-Net located the lungs on the 1024 × 1024 image. Later models worked on lung crops resized to 576 × 576, which the write-up reports to be sufficient.
- **Pseudo-labels.** A ResNet-34 U-Net that scored 0.858 on the public leaderboard predicted masks on images from CheXpert and NIH ChestX-ray14. Positive external images were kept only when the model agreed with them. The write-up gives the ratio of pseudo-labelled to competition samples as 0.5, with equal numbers of positive and negative samples.
- **No classifier and no threshold search.** The solution has no classification model and no classification loss, and uses a fixed threshold of 0.5.
- **Final models.** Three SE-ResNeXt-50 U-Nets with CBAM attention: one at 704 pixels without pseudo-labels, one at 576 pixels with CheXpert pseudo-labels, and one at 576 pixels with CheXpert and NIH pseudo-labels. They were trained with the Lovász loss and Adam at a constant learning rate of $10^{-4}$ for 15 epochs, with an exponential moving average of the weights from epoch 6, and a batch size of 3 per GPU on three GTX 1080 Ti GPUs.

Three of the ten folds of the model with CheXpert pseudo-labels scored 0.8809 public and 0.8642 private. The winner's two submissions scored 0.8679 and 0.8641 private.

## Comparison with other competitions

The winners of the 2022 UW-Madison GI tract segmentation competition credit solutions from this competition for their design of a classification stage followed by a segmentation stage. The metric there also gives a perfect score to an empty prediction on an empty image. The winner's schedule for the sample rate starts training with a high proportion of positive images and ends near the proportion in the test data.

### Considerations for deployment

1. **Chest drains.** A model that responds to chest drains appears accurate on data in which many positive images come from treated patients, and fails on untreated patients. Performance should be measured separately on positive images without a drain.
2. **Operating point.** The thresholds were tuned for a metric in which a false positive on a negative image costs a full point. A triage system may need a different balance between sensitivity and specificity, and the three thresholds should be retuned on local data for that purpose.
3. **Image source.** All images come from one source dataset. Performance on radiographs from other hospitals and equipment should be measured before use.
