## What Nolad documents

Nolad documents winning and near-winning solutions from Kaggle competitions that could plausibly run outside the competition: in a clinic, a laboratory, a school, a phone or a field station. Whether a solution is currently deployed does not matter. What matters is that its inference cost, its dependencies and its licences leave a realistic path to use.

Nolad does not host data, code or model weights. Every report links to the competition data, the authors' write-ups and their repositories, and names the people who built the solution and when. When a better solution appears, the report is updated and the change is dated.

## How competitions are chosen

Candidates are scored from 1 to 5 on five criteria, and the weighted total sets the order in which reports are written.

| Criterion | Weight | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deployability | 30% | Could the winning approach run outside Kaggle under realistic hardware, time and licence constraints? |
| Documentation | 20% | Are there detailed write-ups, released code, weights or a peer-reviewed paper? |
| Impact | 20% | How much does solving the problem matter to people, practitioners or science? |
| Transferable techniques | 15% | Does the solution teach methods that carry over to other problems? |
| Compute accessibility | 15% | Was it trained on hardware a student or small lab can obtain? |

The scores are editorial judgements, recorded with a reason for each so they can be disputed. The full table, including competitions that were considered and set aside, is on the [ranking page](/method/ranking).

## The deployability score

Each report carries a deployability card. The score describes the distance between the competition submission and a system someone could use.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Deployable as released: fast inference on modest hardware, code and weights available under usable licences, no dependence on competition quirks. |
| 4 | Close to deployable: one or two engineering steps remain, such as retraining without non-commercial weights or shrinking an ensemble. |
| 3 | Deployable with work: the core method transfers, but the winning configuration relies on large ensembles, restrictive licences or post-processing tuned to the metric. |
| 2 | Needs substantial engineering: the method is informative but the pipeline would need redesign. |
| 1 | Research artefact: interesting mainly for what it reveals about the data or the metric. |

The card also records inference time and hardware, model count, training cost, external data, the licences of code, data and weights, and the risks a deployer should check first. A strong model whose pretrained weights forbid commercial use scores lower for a company than for a hospital research group; the card states the licence so each reader can judge for their own case.

## How a report is built

Every report starts from the winners' own write-ups and code. The report then adds what those write-ups assume the reader already knows.

1. **Background.** The clinical, scientific, educational or other domain background needed to understand why the data and labels look as they do.
2. **The data.** How it was collected, labelled and split, and the quirks competitors discovered.
3. **The metric.** Its definition, a derivation of what it rewards, and how it shaped the solutions.
4. **The winning solution.** Step by step, in the authors' order, with their numbers.
5. **The mathematics.** Every loss, layer and trick derived without skipped steps, inside collapsible boxes.
6. **The code.** Short excerpts from the authors' repositories with line-by-line explanation and a link to the source.
7. **Other top solutions.** What the runners-up did differently, and what that difference shows.
8. **Compute and deployment.** What the solution costs at Kaggle scale, at laboratory scale and at industrial scale, and where it could be used.
9. **Credits and links.**

A reader who wants the short version reads the summary and the main text. A reader who wants every step opens the boxes. Foundations such as cross-entropy, convolution or attention are written once and embedded in every report that relies on them, so each report can be read on its own without the explanations drifting apart.

## Writing standard

Reports are written in plain scientific prose. They state results and derivations directly, give numbers with their source, and separate what the authors reported from what Nolad infers. Claims that could not be checked against a primary source are marked as such. Rhetorical set-ups, invented questions and statements about what the text is going to do are removed in review.

## Corrections and contributions

Errors are corrected in place and the review date is updated. Competition winners are welcome to review the report on their own solution. Contributions follow the same structure and standard; see the repository README.
